# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

KMU-Inhaber (small and medium business owners) in Hannover and across Germany, actively searching for a web developer to hire. They arrive via search or referral with a clear task: decide within 60 seconds whether Fatih is the right person to build their website.

Their context: they are not developers. They evaluate visually and emotionally first, then by proof. They want to see that Fatih makes websites that win customers — not just websites that look nice. A KMU owner in their 40s or 50s who runs a local trades business, a consulting firm, or a retail store is the canonical visitor.

## Product Purpose

guelerdev.de is Fatih Güler's freelancer portfolio. It exists to convert visitors into inquiries.

Success looks like this: a business owner lands on the site, reads "Websites, die Kunden bringen", recognizes technical credibility and creative distinctiveness, and sends a message via the contact form or email. The site is the pitch deck and the product demonstration simultaneously — the design itself is the evidence that Fatih can do what he claims.

Secondary purpose: signal professionalism and competence to a German-speaking audience that values reliability and Seriosität alongside creativity.

## Brand Personality

Futuristisch · Lebendig · Experimentell

Voice: direkt, kompetent, menschlich. German throughout. No startup hype, no English buzzwords for their own sake. Confident without arrogance — Fatih knows his craft and speaks plainly about it.

Emotional goal: the visitor should feel trust, excitement, and clarity simultaneously. Not one at the cost of the others. The design should feel daring enough to signal creative skill and grounded enough that a traditional business owner trusts Fatih with their brand.

## Anti-references

- Generic Tailwind/shadcn freelancer templates: hero with centered headline + CTA button, three card-grid services, "about me" photo with bullet list of skills, gray-on-white throughout. The modal landing page for the "freelance web developer" category — if it fits that category description without a surprise, restart.
- "I build web apps with passion" portfolio aesthetic — casual, undesigned, clip-art icons, no typographic ambition.
- SaaS metric-hero templates: big numbers, gradient blobs, "10x your revenue" copy. Wrong register entirely.
- Fiverr/Upwork listing pages: transactional, catalog-feel, emphasize cost over craft.
- The editorial-typographic monoculture: display serif italic + small mono labels + ruled separators + monochromatic restraint. By 2026, this lane has saturated the "creative freelancer" category. It reads as trained reflex, not voice. Find the lane that's specifically Fatih's — the Cherenkov Violet / Ionization Cyan palette and the Three.js shader already point somewhere different; follow that signal, not the category default.

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach.** Fatih builds websites that win customers. His own site must win customers. Every design decision is evidence of craft — the homepage IS the portfolio.

2. **Show credibility, don't announce it.** Never write "professional", "experienced", or "passionate". The execution proves it. A visitor who wonders "how was this made?" is already convinced.

3. **German directness over startup hype.** KMU owners want clarity. One strong, specific claim beats five superlatives. "Websites, die Kunden bringen" is the right register — blunt, concrete, benefit-first.

4. **Futuristic without alien.** The aesthetic should signal cutting-edge web craft without intimidating a traditional business owner. Bold, alive, technically impressive — but readable and trustworthy in 60 seconds.

5. **The site is the pitch.** A business owner evaluating Fatih should feel "if my website looked like this, I'd win" within the first scroll. The OrbScene shader, the violet/cyan palette, the typography — all of it should make that argument visually before a word is read.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. German-speaking audience; no RTL or i18n requirements. The audience skews toward business owners in their 40s–60s who may be on consumer-grade hardware or mobile connections — performance matters and type must be legible at size. No reduced-motion animation as a default; respect `prefers-reduced-motion` for all animations.
